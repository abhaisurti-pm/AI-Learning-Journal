# Generative AI

## What is Generative AI?

Generative AI is a branch of artificial intelligence that generates new content based on patterns learned from data and the input/context provided to the model.

It can generate:

- Text
- Images
- Audio
- Video
- Code
- Other forms of content

A simplified model is:

User Input / Prompt + Context
→ Generative AI Model
→ Generated Output

Unlike classification systems, which select from predefined categories, generative AI produces open-ended outputs.

---

## Classification vs Generative AI

### Classification

A classification model maps an input to a predefined category.

Example:

Customer Email
→ AI Model
→ Complaint / Question / Feedback / Other

The output space is predefined.

### Generative AI

A generative model creates new content based on the input and context.

Example:

Customer Complaint
→ Generative AI
→ Draft Customer Response

The output can have many valid variations.

### Key distinction

Classification answers:

> "What is this?"

Generative AI answers:

> "What can be generated based on this?"

This distinction also affects evaluation.

Classification can often be evaluated against known labels and accuracy metrics.

Generative AI requires evaluation of factors such as:

- Accuracy
- Relevance
- Quality
- Tone
- Safety
- Appropriateness
- Usefulness

## Business Applications

Generative AI can be used for:

### Content Creation

- Emails
- Blogs
- Social media posts
- Marketing content
- Presentations
- Product descriptions

### Task Automation

- Meeting summaries
- Report generation
- Document drafting
- Information summarization
- Repetitive content generation

### Customer Interactions

- Customer-support responses
- Chatbots
- Personalized communications
- Assistance for customer-service teams

The important product-management principle is:

> GenAI should solve a meaningful business or user problem rather than being added simply because AI is available.

---

## Generative AI and Business Workflows

A GenAI solution is more than an AI model.

A simplified workflow is:

Business Problem
→ User / Workflow
→ Input + Context
→ AI Model
→ Generated Output
→ Human Review / Action
→ Business Outcome

This means an AI Project Manager must think beyond model selection.

Important questions include:

- What problem are we solving?
- Who is the user?
- What information does the AI need?
- What should the AI generate?
- What does a good output look like?
- What happens when the output is wrong?
- Where is human review required?
- How will success be measured?

---

## Responsible AI

Generative AI systems must be designed and operated responsibly.

Important Responsible AI principles include:

### Fairness

AI systems should provide equitable outcomes and avoid discriminatory behavior.

### Reliability and Safety

AI systems should perform reliably and minimize harmful or unsafe outputs.

### Privacy and Security

User and organizational data must be protected.

Access controls and permissions should ensure that users only receive information they are authorized to access.

### Inclusiveness

AI systems should work effectively for people with different needs and should avoid reinforcing harmful biases.

### Transparency

Users should have an appropriate understanding of how AI is being used and when content is AI-generated.

### Accountability

Organizations and people responsible for AI systems must remain accountable for their impacts.

---

## Human Oversight

Human oversight is particularly important for higher-risk AI use cases.

A simplified workflow is:

AI Generates
→ Human Reviews
→ Human Accepts / Modifies / Rejects
→ Final Action

For example, an AI meeting assistant may generate:

> "Abhay will prepare the client proposal by Friday."

If Abhay never agreed to this, the AI has produced a plausible but incorrect statement.

This should be treated as a product/risk issue rather than simply ignoring the error.

Possible controls include:

- Human confirmation before action items become final
- Clear labeling of AI-generated content
- Ability to edit or reject outputs
- Feedback mechanisms
- Monitoring and evaluation
- Escalation for uncertain or high-risk outputs

---

## AI Project Manager Perspective

An AI Project Manager should not ask only:

> "Can the AI generate this?"

The PM should also ask:

- Should the AI generate this?
- Is the output accurate enough?
- What happens when it is wrong?
- Who is accountable?
- What data does the AI have access to?
- What are the consequences of failure?
- Does a human need to review the output?
- How will the system be evaluated?
- What controls are required before production?

The goal is not maximum automation.

The goal is:

> **Useful AI + appropriate automation + controlled risk + measurable business value**

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Generative AI creates new content based on learned patterns and provided input/context.
2. Classification selects from predefined categories, while generative AI produces open-ended content.
3. GenAI can improve content creation, automation, customer interactions, and business workflows.
4. A GenAI product is more than the underlying model.
5. Responsible AI must be considered throughout the product lifecycle.
6. Human oversight is important when AI outputs can create meaningful consequences.
7. AI output quality must be evaluated, not assumed.
8. An AI Project Manager must manage both business value and AI-specific risks.
